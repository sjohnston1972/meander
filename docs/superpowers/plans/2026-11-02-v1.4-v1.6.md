## Releases and tasks

### v1.4 — the valley's end, a side river, climate, graphs
1. **Valley end: lake and delta** (`src/engine/delta.js`, setting `valleyEnd`: 'open' | 'lake', needs a reset). The last ~1.3 km of the valley is a lake at base level (wavy shore, deepening to 7 m). The river's mouth is the first centreline point over open lake; the river is carved, migrates and carries sediment only above it. The load reaching the mouth builds a delta: deposits spread forward from the mouth(s), filling lake cells to just above the water, so the shore advances and the river lengthens over its own delta. Two distributaries fan out from the delta apex once the delta is big enough and switch now and then (avulsion). Fines partly settle in the lake. Lake mesh, distributary water, Explain labels (delta, distributary, delta lake), HUD line "Building the delta". Lake level follows base level (incision, base-level drops).
2. **Tributary** (`src/engine/tributary.js`, setting `tributary`: its flow as a share of the main river's, 0 = none). Enters from one valley wall (side and place from the seed), winds (Howard–Knutson on its own centreline, neck cutoffs spliced), and joins the main river wherever that is. Below the junction the main river has more water (faster migration, more capacity). The tributary brings a load heavier than its flow can carry: a confluence fan builds, the bed below the junction builds up, and the main river is pushed away from the tributary's side. Water ribbon, labels (tributary, confluence, tributary fan).
3. **Climate swings** (`src/engine/climate.js`, setting `climateSwing` 0–1). Wet and dry periods over centuries (smooth noise, ~1200-year period): flow, flood frequency and sediment supply rise and fall together. Effective settings feed the engine; the HUD names the period.
4. **Graphs over time** (`src/ui/graphs.js`, Show → Graphs). The engine records sinuosity, sediment leaving, bed change and incision, oxbow count, flow and climate every 5 years (thinned when long, saved); a panel draws them as small line charts.
5. Verify, README, review, fix pass, deploy, smoke test.

### v1.5 — grain sorting, log jams, beavers
6. **Grain sorting**: per-point surface gravel fraction. Gravel drops first where the bed builds up (downstream fining, sandy deltas), fines go first where it scours (armour, below dams); armoured beds carry less, sandy beds more. Bars left behind are gravel or sand to match. Labels (armoured bed, sandy bed); cross-section shows the bed.
7. **Log jams** (setting `logJams`): eroding wooded banks feed wood; jams lodge on smaller rivers, back the water up, push the river aside, wet the floodplain above them, rot or wash out in floods. Log piles drawn; label.
8. **Beavers** (setting `beavers`): on rivers and tributaries up to 25 m wide, colonies build dams and ponds; abandoned ponds silt into beaver meadows; oxbows with lodges stay open water longer. Labels.
9. Verify, review, fix, deploy.
