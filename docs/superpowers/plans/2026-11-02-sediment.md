# Sediment transport (v1.3) Implementation Plan

> Executed inline (superpowers:executing-plans). This file is also the ledger.

**Request (Steven, 2026-11-02):** after "where does all the eroded material go" (answer: mostly nowhere — the model had no sediment budget): "build the sediment transport model", then "skip the questions, just do it all".

**Goal:** Eroded material goes somewhere. A sediment budget along the river carries bed material (sand and gravel) from upstream supply, banks, valley walls, landslides and gullies; the bed builds up where more arrives than the river can carry and is scoured where less does (never below bedrock); landslide lakes trap it; the rest leaves the valley and is counted. Fine material (wash load) clouds the water and feeds levees and floodplain silt. An overloaded river aggrades and is more likely to braid; a starved one cuts down.

**Model:** 1D Exner equation along the centreline, transport-limited: capacity Qc = Kt · Q · S (S = local water-surface slope, Q = current discharge incl. floods). Bed change z (`engine.aggr`, metres above the reference profile) solved implicitly each tick (tridiagonal, unconditionally stable): (1−p)·W·ds·dz/dt = F(i−½) − F(i+½) + L(i), F = Kt·Q·(S_ref − dz/ds). Inlet flux = r·capacity, r = 1 + 0.25·(sedimentSupply − 1); outlet z = 0 (base level). Lake (pooled) reaches carry nothing, so sediment drops at the lake head. Bedrock floors scour; aggradation capped. Water level = reference + z.

**Global constraints:** engine determinism (dmath/rng only; determinism guard); a default river (sediment supply 1, no incision) must be bit-identical (golden unchanged — no lateral sources on an ordinary floodplain, zero stays zero); v1.2 saves still open; hostile saves rejected; tick cost growth ≤ ~0.5 ms at Medium.

## Ledger
- Task 1: Ruling: the solver's cap passes what a full bed cannot hold on downstream (and out of the valley), instead of deleting it; scour stopped by bedrock is counted as `clipped` (the river just carries less). In a lake the cap is the lake surface, so a delta builds out into it. Found when Auto braiding never saw more than 1.36x load — the cap was silently deleting sediment.
- Task 3: Ruling: dam test 'wear away' checks survival at 10 years, not 20: a 1.2 m dam's lake (about 190 000 m^3 of channel at the default slope) now fills with sediment in ~17 years, after which the dam is gone; below a dam the river runs clear and scours ~1 m. Both are real dam behaviour — cost if wrong: small landslide dams vanish faster than before.
